The moat is the structure.

Model capability is rented and it gets cheaper every quarter, so nobody holds a position there for long. What compounds is the layer above it: an operation that knows who owns which decision, which agent may touch what, and why one model was chosen over another — with the evidence to show a regulator. That layer is slow to build, and it is what we sell.

What is defensible

Four things that took a year and cannot be bought as a subscription.

An org runtime. Units, decision rights, escalation paths and tool scopes held as live state rather than documentation, and the chart is audited against the running system every day, with any drift shown in the console.

A staffing pipeline. Agents are recruited into defined posts through role cards, training and examination, then certified and placed. Adding a capability is a hiring decision with a record, not a new script in a folder.

Evidence-based routing. Every turn is graded. A model earns a task by out-scoring the alternatives enough times to mean something, and routing stays frozen below that threshold rather than chasing noise.

A published security position. We score our own operation against the NIST AI Risk Management Framework every week and publish it in full, including where we score zero.

Where it sells

Operationally deep, regulated, underserved.
